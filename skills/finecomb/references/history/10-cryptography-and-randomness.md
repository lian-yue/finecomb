# 10 Cryptography and randomness

| Pattern | Historical cases | What to look for | Where it lands |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Fixed or all-zero IV** | Zerologon (CVE-2020-1472, AES-CFB8 with an all-zero IV) | Whether IVs and nonces are really random or unique | [4.4](../specialties/4.4-cryptography-and-credentials.md) ("Uniqueness and single use") |
| Weak randomness source | Debian OpenSSL (CVE-2008-0166); ROCA (CVE-2017-15361, prime generation had structure) | The randomness source and algorithm used for key generation | [4.4](../specialties/4.4-cryptography-and-credentials.md) ("Randomness and unpredictability") |
| Nonce reuse | KRACK (CVE-2017-13077, reinstalling the key reset the nonce); reused signature nonces leak the private key | Whether nonces and counters reset after reconnecting, restarting or restoring a backup | [4.4](../specialties/4.4-cryptography-and-credentials.md) |
| **Downgrade and cross-protocol attacks** | FREAK (CVE-2015-0204); Logjam (CVE-2015-4000); POODLE (CVE-2014-3566); DROWN (CVE-2016-0800, the same key used for an old protocol) | Whether weak algorithms or old versions can be forced during negotiation; whether the same key is used across protocols | [4.1](../specialties/4.1-networking-and-connections.md) ("Transport layer security"), [4.2](../specialties/4.2-protocols-and-frame-parsing.md) ("Version negotiation"), [4.4](../specialties/4.4-cryptography-and-credentials.md) ("Domain separation and purpose separation") |
| Handshake transcript not fully bound | Terrapin (CVE-2023-48795, SSH prefix truncation) | Whether the handshake and sequence numbers are fully authenticated | [4.4](../specialties/4.4-cryptography-and-credentials.md) ("Binding and context", "Protocol state and replay"), [4.2](../specialties/4.2-protocols-and-frame-parsing.md) |
| Compression side channels | CRIME, BREACH | Whether secrets and attacker-controlled data are compressed together and then encrypted | [4.4](../specialties/4.4-cryptography-and-credentials.md) ("Oracles and observable differences") |
| Name resolution poisoning | Kaminsky attack (CVE-2008-1447) | Randomness of transaction IDs and source ports, and response validation, in self-implemented resolvers | [4.1](../specialties/4.1-networking-and-connections.md) ("Self-implemented name resolution") |
| Plaintext-phase data carried into the encrypted phase | Postfix (CVE-2011-0411, plaintext commands buffered before STARTTLS were executed only after encryption was set up) | Whether buffers are cleared at the protocol upgrade point | [4.1](../specialties/4.1-networking-and-connections.md) ("Upgrading to encryption mid-stream") |

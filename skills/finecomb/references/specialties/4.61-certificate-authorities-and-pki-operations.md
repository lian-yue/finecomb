# 4.61 Certificate authorities and PKI operations

Applies to: CA software, ACME servers and clients, internal PKI, and certificate management and automation. Apply your own knowledge of the specific CA software, validation methods and certificate standards to each row.

| Checkpoint | What counts as a problem |
| --- | --- |
| **Domain and identity validation** | Someone who does not control the name can pass validation: it checks from a single network vantage point, follows redirects or aliases to places others control, accepts DNS or routing answers that can be spoofed or hijacked, or skips or misreads CAA; a validation result is reused after it expires or for other names (see [4.62](4.62-dns-servers-and-resolvers.md)) |
| **Issuance authorization** | Anyone who can reach the CA or an enrollment endpoint can request certificates for names, templates or key usages they do not own; the subject or alternative names come from fields the requester can change (see [4.46](4.46-federated-identity-and-single-sign-on.md) ("Identity source when issuing credentials")) |
| **CA key storage** | CA and intermediate private keys sit in files, environment variables or backups instead of hardware protection; signing needs no second person, log or rate limit; root keys meant to be offline are online (see [4.4](4.4-cryptography-and-credentials.md) ("Key lifecycle and custody")) |
| **Profiles and constraints** | Issued certificates get extensions they should not have: the CA flag, a missing or wrong path length, missing name constraints, too broad an extended key usage or too long a validity; extensions from the request are copied into the certificate; verifiers do not enforce the constraints |
| Revocation | Revocation cannot be requested quickly or does not spread; OCSP responses and CRLs are stale, signed by the wrong key or cached too long; relying parties treat an unreachable responder as "not revoked" |
| Certificate transparency | Publicly trusted certificates are not logged; the owner of a name does not watch the logs for certificates issued for it; the logged precertificate differs from the certificate actually issued |
| Renewal and expiry | Renewal is manual or fails silently, so certificates expire and services break or fall back to weaker modes; the same private key is reused on every renewal; credentials used for automation, such as DNS API tokens, can change far more than the records they need |

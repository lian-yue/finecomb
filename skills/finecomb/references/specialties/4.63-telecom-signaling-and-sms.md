# 4.63 Telecom signaling and SMS

Applies to: mobile core networks and signaling (SS7, Diameter, GTP), VoIP and SIP trunks, SMS gateways and aggregators, number provisioning, and SIM and eSIM provisioning. Apply your own knowledge of the specific protocols, network elements and carrier interfaces to each row.

| Checkpoint | What counts as a problem |
| --- | --- |
| **Trust from network position** | Signaling messages, SIP requests or API calls are accepted because they come from an interconnect, a partner network, a trunk or an address range, without checking who sent them or whether that sender may ask about this subscriber; message types that should never come from outside are not filtered at the edge (see [facets](../facets.md) "Where trust comes from") |
| **SMS and calls as proof of identity** | Codes sent by SMS or voice are the only factor, or the recovery path, for valuable accounts, although messages can be redirected or read on the way; codes are not bound to the action they approve (see [4.5](4.5-authentication-sessions-and-tokens.md) ("One-time codes")) |
| **Number porting and SIM swap** | Porting a number, replacing a SIM or moving an eSIM needs only weak checks, such as known personal details or a staff override; services that rely on the number are not told that it moved |
| Location and subscriber lookups | Location, routing and subscriber data can be queried by any interconnect partner or aggregator customer, with no check of purpose and no limit on volume (see [30](../dimensions/30-privacy-data-governance-and-compliance.md)) |
| Toll fraud and premium-rate abuse | Calls or messages can be sent to premium-rate or costly destinations through stolen trunk credentials, open SIP endpoints or verification flows; there are no limits per destination and no spending alarms (see [4.40](4.40-notifications-and-outbound-messages.md) ("SMS and toll fraud")) |
| Caller ID and sender ID spoofing | Caller ID, SMS sender IDs and SIP sender fields are taken as proof of who is calling or sending; customers can set caller numbers or sender IDs they do not own |
| **Provisioning interfaces** | Number, SIM and eSIM provisioning systems and carrier or aggregator APIs have weak authentication, shared credentials or no per-customer scope, so one account can take over or reassign numbers and profiles it does not own; activation codes can be reused |
